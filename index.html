Browser screen recording without installing software

Free Screen Recorder is a no-sign-up screen recorder that runs entirely in your web browser. You capture your display and microphone, optionally add a webcam picture-in-picture overlay, trim and reorder clips, insert title cards, and download one finished WebM video. There is no account wall, no time limit, and no watermark burned into your export. The editor and exporter use standard web APIs—screen capture, MediaRecorder, and canvas rendering—so your footage stays on your computer unless you choose to share it elsewhere.

This tool is part of the Web Free collection of privacy-minded utilities. For a longer walkthrough with tips on permissions and microphone levels, see the hub guide Record your screen with microphone and no watermark. On-site how-tos also cover recording in the browser and using webcam PiP.

How to use this free screen recorder (step by step)

  1. Start screen + mic. Click Start Screen + Mic and pick the window, tab, or full screen the browser is allowed to capture. Grant microphone access when prompted so narration is included in the mix.
  2. Record clips. With capture running, press Start Clip for each segment you want in the final video. Stop the clip when that segment is done. You can record several takes without restarting capture.
  3. Optional webcam PiP. Use Webcam PiP to float a small camera feed over the screen capture—handy for tutorials, stand-ups, and course videos. Adjust the mic offset control if audio and picture feel slightly out of sync.
  4. Edit the timeline. Trim clip in and out points, drag clips to reorder them, and add title blocks with custom background images. You can also import existing video files or reload a saved JSON project from disk.
  5. Export WebM. When the timeline looks right, choose Export Final Video. The app renders locally, shows progress, and offers a download link. Share or edit the WebM in another app if you need MP4 or another format.

Privacy and in-browser processing

Screen recording tools that upload to a cloud encoder may be convenient, but they also copy sensitive UI, credentials, and personal audio to someone else’s servers. Here, capture, preview, trimming, concatenation, and export happen in the tab. Project saves download as a file on your device; we do not operate a backend that receives your recordings. Like any website, this page may load ads and anonymous analytics tags, but those services are separate from the recorder pipeline—your video bytes are not sent to us for encoding. Use a secure context (HTTPS) as required by browser capture APIs.

Popular use cases

  • Software tutorials — Show a product workflow with voice-over and optional face cam via PiP.
  • Bug reports — Capture reproduction steps for engineering tickets without installing OBS.
  • Short training clips — Record chapterized clips, trim mistakes, and export one file for LMS upload.
  • Meeting highlights — Grab a tab or window segment with mic commentary for async teammates.
  • Content drafts — Record rough takes and title sections before heavier editing elsewhere.

Chromium-based browsers on desktop offer the most reliable capture, PiP, and export. Safari and Firefox vary; if export fails, try Chromium or see the about page. Most phones block full screen capture—use a desktop for recording.

Frequently asked questions

Is Free Screen Recorder really free?
Yes. There is no subscription, watermark, or time limit. Export stays in your browser as WebM.
Does my video get uploaded?
No. Capture, editing, and export run locally. Project files download as JSON on your device only.
Which browsers work best?
Modern Chromium browsers on desktop are recommended. Safari and Firefox may lack full export or PiP support.
Can I record on my phone?
Layout works at mobile widths, but most phones block full screen capture in the browser. Use desktop for recording.
Can I trim clips and export one WebM file?
Yes. Record multiple segments, trim and reorder them, add optional title cards, then export a single WebM file rendered entirely in your browser.
